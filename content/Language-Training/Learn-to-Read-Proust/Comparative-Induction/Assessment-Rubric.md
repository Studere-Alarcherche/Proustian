# Assessment Rubric

This rubric assesses whether a page trains high-order reading through comparative induction.

It does not grade literary knowledge. It checks whether perception has been trained.

## Core Criteria

| Criterion | Strong | Weak | Drift |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recherche center | Begins from Proust and returns with sharper perception | Mentions Proust but does not return clearly | Comparative author becomes the center |
| Passage rigor | Works at passage level with bounded anchors | Uses vague textual reference | Produces summaries or thematic claims |
| Induction pressure | Makes the reader infer patterns through repeated actions | Explains the pattern too early | Gives abstract theory first |
| Difference amplifier | Uses comparison to reveal Proustian structure | Compares traits side by side | Treats comparison as literary tourism |
| Reading actions | Requires marking, tracing, comparing, and restating | Includes passive questions | Offers appreciation without action |
| Multilingual transfer | Observes mutation across French, English, and Chinese where relevant | Mentions languages without pressure | Becomes translation evaluation |
| Structural restatement | Produces a mechanism in the reader's own words | Produces paraphrase | Produces theme or summary |
| Return movement | Ends by sharpening Recherche | Ends with the comparative author | Ends with general theory |

## Required Pass Conditions

A page passes only if it contains:

- a bounded Recherche anchor
- a named Proustian pressure
- a comparative pressure that serves Recherche
- at least three concrete reading actions
- a structural restatement
- an explicit return-to-Proust movement

## Self-Correction Rules

If the comparative author occupies more attention than Recherche, reduce the comparative material and strengthen the return.

If the page explains before the reader has acted, move abstraction later.

If the page becomes thematic, replace theme language with sentence movement, temporal logic, rhythm, and perceptual regime.

If the page becomes translation evaluation, ask what literary perception survives, mutates, compresses, expands, or reorganizes across languages.

If the page becomes a survey, cut coverage and return to one passage pressure.

## Reader Formation Check

The final test is simple:

Can the reader now perceive a structure in Recherche that was harder to perceive before the comparison?

If not, the page is not complete.
