# Ages

Ages are not chronological periods. They are perception regimes.

An Age names the kind of readerly perception a passage asks for. It should help the reader notice how reality is made visible, delayed, fractured, condensed, or reorganized.

## Age Record Template

Each Age should be recorded with:

- Age name
- perception regime
- sentence behavior
- temporal behavior
- multilingual pressure
- Proustian return question

## Initial Ages

## Age of Delayed Recognition

Perception regime:

- meaning arrives after expansion, delay, reclassification, and retrospective pressure.

Sentence behavior:

- subordinate movement, suspended arrival, qualification, delayed main force.

Temporal behavior:

- the present becomes readable through later return.

Proustian return question:

- What does the passage ask the reader to hold before it can be understood?

## Age of Flowing Interior Weather

Perception regime:

- consciousness changes as rhythm, atmosphere, and shifting inner surface.

Sentence behavior:

- waves, transitions, and mobile perceptions.

Temporal behavior:

- duration appears as felt continuity.

Proustian return question:

- Where does Proust interrupt flow with belated recognition?

## Age of Broken Time

Perception regime:

- reality appears through fracture, recurrence, interruption, and non-linear pressure.

Sentence behavior:

- discontinuity, recursion, abrupt temporal shifts.

Temporal behavior:

- sequence becomes unstable.

Proustian return question:

- How does Proust reorganize time without making fracture the final form?

## Age of Reflective Distance

Perception regime:

- reality is held at historical, ethical, or retrospective distance.

Sentence behavior:

- measured reflection, conceptual poise, syntactic control.

Temporal behavior:

- time becomes intelligible through later judgment.

Proustian return question:

- How does Recherche differ when reflection emerges from sensation rather than preceding it?

## Age of Recomposition

Perception regime:

- meaning survives transfer only by being rebuilt, not carried intact.

Sentence behavior:

- pressure changes across French, English, and Chinese.

Temporal behavior:

- the reader experiences delay again during recomposition.

Proustian return question:

- What must be rebuilt for Proustian perception to survive in another language?
