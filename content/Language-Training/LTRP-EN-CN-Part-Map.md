# LTRP EN-CN Part Map

The textbook has eight parts. Each part will eventually contain six chapters. This map defines the global structure and drafts only the Part 1 chapter outline.

## Part 1 — How a Long Sentence Delays Thought

- Part thesis: Proustian reading begins by learning how a sentence delays arrival. The reader must stay inside suspension before extracting meaning.
- Core reading pressure: Long sentences hold memory, perception, and qualification in motion before the main claim stabilizes.
- EN-CN training focus: Track delay, insertion, subordination, and return; then decide how Chinese can preserve movement without producing unreadable imitation.
- Expected chapter type: Sentence movement chapter with one passage anchor and one movement card.
- Output artifact: Delay Movement Card.

   Function: Train the reader to notice how expectation is created before completion.

   Function: Compare close and interpretive Chinese recomposition strategies for preserving temporal pressure.

## Part 2 — How Memory Enters Through Syntax

- Part thesis: Memory in Proust is not only a theme; it is staged by syntactic return, interruption, and delayed recognition.
- Core reading pressure: The sentence often makes memory arrive as a structural event rather than as a stated idea.
- EN-CN training focus: Track how English marks recurrence, tense pressure, and recognition; test how Chinese can recompose return without flattening it.
- Expected chapter type: Memory-syntax chapter with a passage anchor and one recognition card.
- Output artifact: Memory Entry Card.

## Part 3 — Why Literal Translation Fails Proust

- Part thesis: Literal translation often preserves words while losing movement, pressure, and hierarchy.
- Core reading pressure: A correct word can be wrong if it destroys the sentence's unfolding.
- EN-CN training focus: Compare close rendering, interpretive rendering, and recomposition; name concrete loss and gain.
- Expected chapter type: Translation-judgment chapter.
- Output artifact: Loss / Gain Judgment Card.

## Part 4 — How English Rebuilds French Rhythm

- Part thesis: English reading must rebuild rhythm as a sequence of attention, not as a decorative echo of French.
- Core reading pressure: Rhythm is carried by order, stress, punctuation, and recurrence.
- EN-CN training focus: Use English sentence movement as a bridge for understanding what Chinese must later remake.
- Expected chapter type: English movement chapter.
- Output artifact: English Rhythm Card.

## Part 5 — How Chinese Must Recompose Perception

- Part thesis: Chinese translation of Proust requires recomposition of perception, not mechanical transfer.
- Core reading pressure: Chinese must decide what to make explicit, what to delay, what to repeat, and what to let remain suspended.
- EN-CN training focus: Test Chinese segmentation, topic flow, verbal pressure, and sensory continuity.
- Expected chapter type: Chinese recomposition chapter.
- Output artifact: Chinese Recomposition Card.

## Part 6 — The Sentence as a Chamber of Time

- Part thesis: A Proustian sentence can become a chamber where perception, memory, and judgment coexist.
- Core reading pressure: Time is not merely narrated; it is built into the sentence's spatial and syntactic arrangement.
- EN-CN training focus: Preserve temporal layering while keeping the translated sentence readable.
- Expected chapter type: Time-chamber chapter.
- Output artifact: Temporal Chamber Card.

## Part 7 — Translation as Writing Training

- Part thesis: Translation becomes useful when it trains prose judgment and sentence-making.
- Core reading pressure: The reader must decide how reading pressure can become writing practice without imitating Proust superficially.
- EN-CN training focus: Convert movement analysis into controlled English or Chinese prose exercises.
- Expected chapter type: Writing-transfer chapter.
- Output artifact: Writing Move Card.

## Part 8 — Reading Proust to Learn Prose

- Part thesis: The final aim is not translation performance but a stronger prose intelligence formed through Proustian reading.
- Core reading pressure: Reading, judgment, recomposition, and writing must return to the reader's own sentences.
- EN-CN training focus: Build reusable prose practices from earlier movement cards.
- Expected chapter type: Integration chapter.
- Output artifact: Prose Training Portfolio.

## Open Constraint

No full chapter sequence should be generated until actual passage anchors and user work exist.
