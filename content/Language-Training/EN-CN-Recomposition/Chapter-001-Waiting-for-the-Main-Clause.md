# Chapter 1｜Waiting for the Main Clause

## 1. What You Train

> [!note] What You Train
> Waiting for the main clause.
>
> You are training how to hold attention while a sentence delays completion.

## 2. Core Explanation

> [!note] Core Explanation
> A long Proustian sentence may ask you to wait before deciding what it means. Do not translate from the first word as if the sentence has already completed itself.
>
> For this chapter, the first task is simple: find where orientation arrives late. Only after that should you draft Chinese.

> [!warning] Do Not Ask For This Yet
> Do not ask AI for a final translation.
>
> Do not read the Teacher Note before your own recognition and recomposition attempt.

## 3. Input

Source: Marcel Proust, *Swann's Way*, Volume One of *Remembrance of Things Past* / *In Search of Lost Time*

Version: C. K. Scott Moncrieff English translation, Project Gutenberg eBook #7178, 1922

Location: Overture, opening sleep sequence

Start Words: For a long time

End Words: a matter dark indeed

Passage status: selected

### Source Passage

> For a long time I used to go to bed early. Sometimes, when I had put out my candle, my eyes would close so quickly that I had not even time to say "I'm going to sleep." And half an hour later the thought that it was time to go to sleep would awaken me; I would try to put away the book which, I imagined, was still in my hands, and to blow out the light; I had been thinking all the time, while I was asleep, of what I had just been reading, but my thoughts had run into a channel of their own, until I myself seemed actually to have become the subject of my book: a church, a quartet, the rivalry between François I and Charles V. This impression would persist for some moments after I was awake; it did not disturb my mind, but it lay like scales upon my eyes and prevented them from registering the fact that the candle was no longer burning. Then it would begin to seem unintelligible, as the thoughts of a former existence must be to a reincarnate spirit; the subject of my book would separate itself from me, leaving me free to choose whether I would form part of it or no; and at the same time my sight would return and I would be astonished to find myself in a state of darkness, pleasant and restful enough for the eyes, and even more, perhaps, for my mind, to which it appeared incomprehensible, without a cause, a matter dark indeed.

## 10. Teacher Note

> [!warning] Read after attempting
> The following Teacher Note is not the starting point.
>
> Use it only after you have completed your recognition drill, rough Chinese recomposition, and AI feedback step.

### Sentence Skeleton

Use this skeleton before any recomposition:

Main clause: "I used to go to bed early"; later local main actions include "the thought ... would awaken me," "I would try," "This impression would persist," and "my sight would return."

Main verb: "used to go"; "would awaken"; "would try"; "would persist"; "would return."

Delayed element: Stable waking orientation is delayed until "my sight would return."

Inserted phrase(s): "when I had put out my candle"; "I imagined"; "while I was asleep"; "pleasant and restful enough for the eyes."

Subordinate clause(s): "when I had put out my candle"; "that I had not even time"; "which, I imagined, was still in my hands"; "until I myself seemed."

Temporal marker(s): "For a long time"; "Sometimes"; "And half an hour later"; "all the time"; "Then"; "at the same time."

Sensory unit(s): candle, eyes closing, scales upon the eyes, darkness.

Return point: "my sight would return."

Completion: The passage completes when darkness becomes "pleasant and restful" yet mentally "incomprehensible."

Working rule: if the main verb is not located, translation has not begun. It has only guessed.

### Movement Map

Opening: "For a long time" creates habitual duration before any specific scene.

Delay: Sleep, reading, waking, and mental residue interrupt any simple statement of going to bed.

Expansion: Book, historical subject, church, quartet, rivalry, candle, eyes, and darkness gather into one mental field.

Suspension: The reader cannot yet say "he wakes up in darkness" without losing the passage's drift through reading and half-sleep.

Return: "my sight would return" brings the mind back from book-subject to room.

Completion: The darkness is not merely physical; it becomes the residue of delayed orientation.

The movement is not simply "long sentence = difficult grammar." The sentence teaches patience by forcing attention to remain incomplete.

Reading use:

- Do not begin Chinese recomposition at `B` alone.
- The training unit is the whole movement from habitual time to delayed orientation.
- The key delay is not only grammatical; it is perceptual: the narrator returns to the room late.

### English Style Note

In English, a delayed main clause changes rhythm and expectation. The sentence asks the reader to breathe through dependent material before receiving the grammatical center.

Watch for:

- rhythm: where the sentence asks for a pause without ending
- delay: where the expected main movement is withheld
- breath: whether the sentence can be read in one held movement or needs internal staging
- clause movement: which clauses depend on which center
- perception: whether perception accumulates before explanation
- abstraction: whether abstract language appears before or after concrete detail
- emphasis: what becomes important because it arrives late

The style lesson is to read arrival as an event. The main clause matters partly because it has been delayed.

### Chinese Recomposition Note

Literal Chinese often flattens this pattern by moving the main clause too early. That can make the sentence clearer but less Proustian.

Preserve:

- the reader's waiting
- the pressure of incomplete syntax
- the order in which perception gathers
- the late arrival of the governing action or judgment

Decide:

- Can Chinese keep one long sentence without becoming stiff?
- Should the sentence be divided into two or three units while preserving delayed arrival?
- Which element must remain late?
- Which inserted material can move earlier without destroying the pattern?

Do not produce a final "correct translation" until the source passage is selected. For now, practice building a Chinese draft that marks the main clause and keeps the delay visible.

### Pattern Explanation

Pattern name: PATTERN-Delayed-Main-Clause

Type: English Sentence Pattern

What it does: Postpones the governing clause so the reader must hold attention before the sentence becomes complete.

How to recognize it: The sentence opens with dependent or descriptive material, while the main verb or central claim arrives late.

How to practice it: Underline the main clause, bracket delaying material, then draft Chinese only after the sentence center is visible.

Proustian function: Delay turns reading into an experience of waiting, adjustment, and delayed perception.

Chinese adaptation: Preserve delayed arrival where possible; divide only when division keeps the original movement.
