# Question Gradient

## Root Question

How does Proust train the reader to discover truth through signs, sensation, attention, misrecognition, and artistic form?

## Starting Questions

- What sign appears before it is understood?
- What kind of sensation starts the reading process?
- What kind of delay, mistake, or misrecognition organizes the scene?
- What secondary lens clarifies the passage without replacing Proust?
- What form of attention does the passage train?
- How can this passage become a reader-facing card?

## U1｜Signs / Method

How does Proust train the reader to read signs rather than merely follow plot, psychology, or memory?

Supporting questions:
- What forces thought before interpretation begins?
- What makes a sign readable?
- How does Deleuze help without replacing Proust?

## U2｜Memory / Time / Space

How does Proust transform memory from recollection into a structure of signs, time, space, and artistic discovery?

Supporting questions:
- How does the Madeleine scene turn sensation into discovery?
- How does involuntary memory reopen time through place and sensation?
- How does habit shape what can or cannot be perceived?

## U3｜Music / Ineffable

How does Vinteuil’s music move from emotional sign to artistic truth, ineffable experience, and novelistic structure?

Supporting questions:
- How does music carry truth before it can be explained?
- How does the little phrase move from love sign to art sign?
- What does the ineffable allow the novel to think?

## U4｜Visuality / Gaze / Photography / Attention

How does Proust transform seeing into a discipline of attention, misrecognition, capture, and artistic truth?

Supporting questions:
- How does the Vermeer yellow wall become a test of attention?
- How does seeing differ from understanding?
- How do gaze, framing, and capture organize misrecognition?

## U5｜Space / Sensory History

How do spaces and sensory environments in Proust produce social time and historical subjectivity?

Supporting questions:
- How do rooms and salons become social machines?
- How do bells gather collective time?
- How do smell and atmosphere mark social boundaries?

## U6｜Writing Apparatus

How can U1–U5 be transformed into teachable, writable, reader-facing forms without losing aesthetic and intellectual force?

Supporting questions:
- How does a Passage Card become a reader-facing block?
- How does a Unit Digest become a course page?
- How does the website remain a reading garden rather than a content dump?
