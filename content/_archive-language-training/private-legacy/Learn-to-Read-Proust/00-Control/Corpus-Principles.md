# Corpus Principles

The course should use Proust as its source horizon from the beginning.

Use:

- Proust-oriented vocabulary
- Proust-oriented model sentences
- short adapted fragments
- controlled sentences modeled on Proustian syntax
- later, carefully selected source passages

Avoid:

- unrelated everyday French
- long quotations in early chapters
- literary essay commentary in grammar chapters
- vocabulary that does not prepare the reader for Proust

Source-status labels:

- `attested`: directly present in the source passage being used.
- `adapted`: shortened or adjusted from source material for teaching.
- `Proust-oriented`: selected because it prepares Proust reading, but not claimed as a quotation.
- `course-control`: supplied by the course for controlled practice.

Early chapters may use simple controlled sentences. Later chapters should increasingly return to authentic Proustian phrases, clauses, and periods.
