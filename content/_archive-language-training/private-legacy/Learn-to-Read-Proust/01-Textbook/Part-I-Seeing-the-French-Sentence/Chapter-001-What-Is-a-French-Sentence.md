# Chapter 001 — What Is a French Sentence?

## 1. Chapter Aim

Learn to see a French sentence as an ordered unit before interpretation.

At this level, a controlled French sentence usually gives:

- a subject group
- a conjugated verb
- sometimes, a completion

The first task is not literary interpretation. The first task is structural sight.

## 2. Proustian Reading Target

Proust remains the final target, but Chapter 001 is not direct Proust reading.

This chapter trains the first habit needed for Proust: pause before interpreting, locate the sentence, and identify its main movement.

## 3. Core Grammar

A French sentence begins with a capital letter and usually ends with a period, question mark, or exclamation mark.

In a very simple sentence, the subject group normally comes before the conjugated verb.

Controlled pattern for this chapter:

Subject group + conjugated verb + completion, if there is one

Key terms:

| Term | Working meaning for Chapter 001 |
| --- | --- |
| sentence | a complete written unit |
| subject group | who or what the sentence is about |
| conjugated verb | the verb form that carries the sentence movement |
| completion | a temporary reading label for a word or group that completes the thought |

Some simple training sentences have no completion.

Do not translate too quickly. First locate the structure.

## 4. Forms / Paradigm

For now, recognize only these present-tense forms.

| Infinitive | Form | Tense / person / number | Reading value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| regarder | regarde | present, 3rd person singular | looks |
| revenir | revient | present, 3rd person singular | returns / comes back |
| devenir | devient | present, 3rd person singular | becomes |
| apparaître | apparaît | present, 3rd person singular | appears |

Note: `regarde` is used here as a controlled no-object form for recognition. Later chapters will add ordinary object patterns such as "looks at something."

## 6. Model Sentences

1. Le narrateur regarde.
2. La chambre apparaît.
3. Le souvenir revient.
4. Le temps devient une phrase.
5. Une sensation apparaît.

These are controlled training sentences, not Proust quotations. They train sentence recognition, not literary interpretation.

## 7. Guided Parsing

Reusable parsing formula:

Full sentence / subject group / verb form / completion or no completion / basic reading

Sentence: Le souvenir revient.

| Step | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What is the full sentence? | Le souvenir revient. |
| 2 | What is the subject group? | le souvenir |
| 3 | What is the verb form? | revient |
| 4 | Is there a completion? | no completion |
| 5 | What is the basic reading? | the memory returns |

Sentence: Le temps devient une phrase.

| Step | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What is the full sentence? | Le temps devient une phrase. |
| 2 | What is the subject group? | le temps |
| 3 | What is the verb form? | devient |
| 4 | What is the completion? | une phrase |
| 5 | What is the basic reading? | time becomes a sentence |

## 8. English Structural Control

English is the scaffold language for this course.

Use English to control structure before interpretation:

| French task | English control question |
| --- | --- |
| find the sentence | What is the complete sentence? |
| find the subject group | Who or what is the sentence about? |
| find the verb form | What is the conjugated verb? |
| check completion | Does the verb need a completion here? |
| give a basic reading | What is the plain English structural sense? |

English control is not free literary translation. It is a way to keep the French structure visible.

## 10. Review

Before interpreting, identify:

- the full sentence
- the subject group
- the conjugated verb
- the completion, if there is one
- the literal English control
- the natural English paraphrase

The first discipline is seeing the French sentence.
