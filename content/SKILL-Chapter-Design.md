# SKILL-Chapter-Design

## Purpose

Convert one knowledge point into a formal textbook chapter.

This skill keeps Learn to Read French / Learn to Read Proust from becoming a loose essay, vocabulary dump, or generic French lesson. Each chapter must teach one visible reading action and connect it to cards, self-test, diagnosis, and Proustian alignment.

## When to Use

- when drafting LTF chapters
- when revising a weak chapter
- when transforming notes into textbook form
- when checking whether a chapter can support future card and diagnostic work

## Inputs

- chapter number
- chapter title and subtitle
- target reading problem
- intended vocabulary set
- intended forms or patterns
- Proustian reading pressure
- card and self-test targets

## Procedure

Every chapter must include:

1. Chapter Aim
2. Reading Problem
3. Core Knowledge
4. Vocabulary
5. Forms and Patterns
6. Model Sentences
7. Reading Method
8. Proustian Alignment
9. Self-Test Link
10. Card Targets
11. Diagnostic Notes
12. Pedagogical Audit

## Audit Questions

- Does the chapter train one main reading action?
- Does the chapter have enough knowledge density to function as a textbook page?
- Is the vocabulary selected for reading utility?
- Are forms and patterns explicit enough?
- Are the model sentences short and controlled for the level?
- Does the chapter point to cards and self-test?
- Does the Proustian alignment avoid generic French and premature difficulty?

## Failure Signals

- reads like an essay, not a textbook
- no clear chapter aim
- too much theory before reading action
- vocabulary is random or insufficient
- no card targets
- no self-test pathway
- Proustian connection is decorative
