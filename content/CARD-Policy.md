# Card Policy

Cards repeat what the textbook has already taught.

Cards do not teach new grammar by themselves.

Good cards are small, answerable, and tied to reading.

Cards are the retrieval engine of the textbook. They convert chapter knowledge into reviewable memory.

One card should test one knowledge point.

Avoid:

- large translation prompts
- literary interpretation cards
- cards with multiple possible answers
- cards that require grammar not yet introduced

Use cards for:

- forms
- articles
- gender and number
- function words
- false friends
- sentence skeletons
- Proust-oriented micro-patterns

## Reliability Rules

- If a card is too vague to review, split it or delete it.
- If a card tests appreciation rather than recall, recognition, form, function, or parsing, rewrite it.
- If a card fails repeatedly, diagnose whether the issue is the card, the chapter, or the learner's retrieval gap.
- Cloze cards should be used only where deletion helps retrieval.
