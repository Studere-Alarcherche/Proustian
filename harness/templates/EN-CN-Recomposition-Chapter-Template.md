# EN-CN Recomposition Chapter Template

Use this backstage template for EN-CN Recomposition chapters. Do not publish a chapter generated from this template until it has been reviewed.

## What You Train

Name the sentence movement or recomposition action.

Examples:

- Waiting for the main clause
- Inserted phrases and suspended attention
- Delay as a form of thought
- Return after perceptual expansion

## Core Explanation

Explain the movement briefly.

Do not provide:

- final translation
- full movement map
- full skeleton
- final interpretation

Use a warning callout before learner work:

```markdown
> [!warning] Do Not Ask For This Yet
> Do not ask AI for a final translation.
> Do not read the Teacher Note before your own attempt.
```

## Input

Provide a short passage anchor or safe excerpt.

## Required Practice Elements

Each chapter must contain:

1. **Primary Work Zone**
   Choose one sentence, passage zone, or anchor. Do not ask the learner to work on the whole passage at once.
2. **Recognition Drill**
   The learner marks something visible: verb, return point, insertion, extension, sensory contact, or completion point.
3. **Recomposition Drill**
   The learner writes a rough Chinese attempt before AI feedback.
4. **Repair Drill**
   The learner repairs one local issue only.
5. **Thinking Question**
   The learner answers one judgment question, not a recall question.
6. **Transfer Drill**
   The learner applies the same move to a new sentence, new passage, or original Chinese sentence.
7. **Movement Card**
   The chapter ends with one lightweight card.

Practice must create friction inside language, not administrative burden outside language.

## Recognition Drill

Before AI feedback, the learner marks or identifies the structure:

1. Mark where the sentence seems to complete.
2. Mark the main clause or main movement.
3. Mark what delays, interrupts, extends, or suspends completion.
4. Write one short note naming the pressure.

## Recomposition Drill

Before AI feedback, the learner writes a rough Chinese attempt:

1. Do not make it elegant.
2. Preserve the chapter pattern.
3. Mark where the Chinese completes the meaning.
4. Note where the Chinese may become too smooth too early.

## AI Feedback Prompt

Use:

```text
I will give you my attempt first.

Do not give a final translation.
Do not rewrite the whole passage for me.

Evaluate my attempt as a training draft.

Focus on:
1. whether I preserved the sentence movement;
2. whether I completed the meaning too early;
3. whether the Chinese recomposition flattened the delay;
4. the one repair I should make now.
```

## Repair / Retry if needed

The learner repairs one local problem.

No forced second attempt. No artificial failure. If the first attempt is strong, proceed to Card.

## Thinking Question

Ask one judgment question that cannot be answered by recall alone.

Examples:

- What changes if Chinese completes the meaning earlier than English does?
- Which is more important here: informational accuracy or preserving delayed perception?
- Where does your version become too smooth, and what does that smoothness destroy?

## Transfer Drill

The learner transfers the same movement to a new sentence or one original Chinese sentence.

## Teacher Note

Only now provide:

- sentence movement
- delayed structure
- key recomposition pressure
- one short model fragment if necessary

## Card

Create one Movement Card:

```markdown
# Movement Card｜[Name]

## Source

Short anchor / passage location

## My Try

One sentence about my first handling.

## AI Feedback

Max 3 points.

## Repair

What I changed.

## Pattern

The sentence movement.

## CN Recomposition Rule

How Chinese should preserve the pressure.

## Reuse

How to use this in the next passage or in my own prose.
```

## Reuse Task

Write one original Chinese sentence that uses the same movement.
