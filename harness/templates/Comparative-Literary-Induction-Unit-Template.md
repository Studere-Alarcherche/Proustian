# Comparative Literary Induction Unit Template

Use this backstage template for Comparative Literary Induction units. Do not publish a unit generated from this template until it has been reviewed.

## 1. Unit Question

State the trainable literary problem.

Example: How does delayed syntax become a method of social perception?

## 2. Proust Anchor

Provide a short Proust anchor or source location.

Do not expand quotation length unnecessarily.

## 3. English-Literary Counterpoint

Name the comparative author, text, scene, sentence, or critical tradition.

Keep the counterpoint tied to the unit question.

## 4. Brief Context

Give only the context needed to begin reading.

Do not turn the unit into an encyclopedia entry.

## 5. Close Reading Drill

The learner marks the Proust anchor before receiving synthesis.

Examples:

- mark where syntax delays knowledge
- mark a social perception cue
- mark the phrase that changes judgment
- mark what translation makes more or less visible

## 6. Comparative Drill

The learner compares one precise movement before AI feedback.

Examples:

- compare how Proust and the counterpoint delay knowledge
- compare how each text handles memory
- compare how each sentence stages perception
- compare prose rhythm after translation

## 7. Critical Thinking Question

Ask one judgment question.

Example: Is delay merely style here, or is it a form of knowing?

## 8. Writing Prompt

Write a short critical paragraph before asking AI for synthesis.

Default length: 200-300 words.

## 9. AI Feedback Prompt

```text
I will give you my close reading or comparison attempt first.

Do not write the critical judgment for me.
Do not turn this into a finished essay.

Challenge my comparison as a training draft.

Focus on:
1. whether I used textual evidence precisely;
2. whether my comparison is too broad;
3. whether Proust remains the center;
4. the one repair or counterargument I should address now.

Give no more than 3 feedback points, one repair instruction, and one thinking question.
```
