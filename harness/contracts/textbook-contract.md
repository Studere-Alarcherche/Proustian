# Textbook Contract

Textbook and teaching-material production must follow Harness before publication.

The learner remains sovereign. AI must never perform the central learning act before the learner attempts it.

Explain lightly. Train heavily. Feedback precisely. Record minimally.

## Allowed States

- idea
- briefed
- drafted
- testing
- reviewed
- revising
- stable
- published
- archived

## Public Exposure Rule

Stable before public file.

Published before public navigation.

Archived means preserved but retired.

Revised templates and contracts remain backstage until a chapter is explicitly reviewed for public exposure.

Public language pages must not make the material look more complete than it is.

## Human-First Validity Rule

No chapter is valid if AI performs the central learning act before the learner attempts it.

Basic explanation is still necessary, but it must be concise, functional, and subordinate to training.

The learner does not need to fail artificially. The learner must attempt genuinely. Revision is optional and depends on feedback. The final artifact is one lightweight card. The system may preserve context backstage, but the learner-facing action must stay light.

## Required Chapter Model

Every revised chapter should follow this model:

1. What You Train
2. Core Explanation
3. Input
4. Try
5. AI Feedback
6. Repair / Retry if needed
7. Teacher Note / Pattern Explanation
8. Card
9. Reuse

For EN-CN recomposition chapters, use the denser training model:

1. Core Explanation
2. Input
3. Recognition Drill
4. Recomposition Drill
5. AI Feedback
6. Repair Drill
7. Thinking Question
8. Transfer Drill
9. Teacher Note / Pattern Explanation
10. Movement Card

Core Explanation must be short and functional. It must not include a final translation, full skeleton, full movement map, or final interpretation before the learner attempts the task.

Input must be small enough to act on:

- a sentence
- a phrase
- a short passage
- a citation anchor
- a pattern

Try must require the learner to perform the central action first:

- mark the main clause
- find the subject and conjugated verb
- draft a rough Chinese version
- use the phrase in one sentence
- identify where orientation arrives
- mark what must be delayed

AI Feedback may answer only:

1. What is strongest in the attempt?
2. What is the most important weakness?
3. What is one concrete repair?
4. What question should the learner answer before continuing?

Repair / Retry is conditional. If the first attempt is strong, proceed to Card.

Teacher Note / Pattern Explanation comes only after the learner attempt and feedback. Only then may the chapter provide:

- sentence skeleton
- movement map
- recomposition principle
- grammar explanation
- pattern summary

## AI Feedback Rules

AI feedback must be short and must not overwhelm the learner.

Default feedback cap:

- maximum 3 feedback points
- one repair instruction
- one question

Allowed AI roles:

- material provider
- coach
- corrector
- example generator
- card compressor

Forbidden AI roles before learner attempt:

- final translator
- complete parser
- movement-map provider
- full interpretation provider
- answer-key provider
- chapter author replacing learner action

## Card Rule

Each chapter produces one lightweight card.

The card should be short, reusable, and not administratively heavy.

Optional backstage state may record more context, but learner-facing work should stay limited to:

Try -> Feedback -> Repair / Retry if needed -> Card

## Comparative Literary Induction Units

Comparative Literary Induction is a real textbook line, not a loose backstage holding area.

It treats Proust in English translation as part of the English literary world and uses comparative literary exercises, close reading questions, and critical writing prompts to train judgment.

A unit may learn from Norton-style literary pedagogy:

- brief historical or literary context
- primary Proust anchor
- English-literary counterpoint
- close reading questions
- comparison drills
- critical debate prompts
- short writing assignments
- AI feedback after learner attempt
- final concept / comparison card

The human-first rule applies: the learner must attempt close reading or comparison before AI gives synthesis. AI may challenge, clarify, and suggest counterarguments, but must not write the critical judgment first.

## Required Chapter Questions

- What reading move does this chapter train?
- What must the learner recognize after this chapter?
- What errors are likely?
- What examples are used?
- What is the mini training test?
- What must the learner attempt before AI gives the fuller answer?
- What one repair should feedback make possible?
- What one card should remain after the chapter?
- What recognition, production/recomposition, repair, transfer, and thinking-question work does the chapter require?
- Has this been actually practiced?
- Is it public-ready?
