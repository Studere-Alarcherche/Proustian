# Comparative Literary Induction Contract

Comparative Literary Induction is the third textbook line of the Proustian language layer.

It is not a generic comparative-literature encyclopedia.

It treats Proust in English translation as part of the English literary world and uses comparative literary exercises to train close reading, comparative judgment, and critical writing.

## Function

This line trains the reader to:

- read Proust as translated literature inside English literary culture
- understand how translated literature enters English reading, teaching, comparison, and criticism
- compare Proust with English and American writers through precise literary problems
- practice close reading, comparative reading, critical debate, and short critical writing
- form judgment about Proust's position in world literature and the English literary classroom

## Pedagogical Model

Comparative Literary Induction may learn from Norton-style literary pedagogy:

- brief historical or literary context
- primary Proust anchor
- English-literary counterpoint
- close reading questions
- comparison drills
- critical debate prompts
- short writing assignments
- AI feedback after learner attempt
- final concept / comparison card

## Human-First Rule

The learner must attempt close reading or comparison before AI gives synthesis.

AI may:

- challenge a comparison
- clarify a weak distinction
- suggest counterarguments
- identify missing textual evidence
- compress a finished attempt into a concept card

AI must not:

- write the critical judgment first
- provide the finished comparison before the learner attempts it
- turn author lists into a public maze
- replace Proust as the mother star

## Public Boundary

This line is backstage / working by default.

Selected units may become public only after they are stable, reader-facing, and reviewed as finished artifacts.

Do not expose Comparative Literary Induction through public navigation until selected units are ready.

## Comparison Boundary

Comparison must center on a trainable problem, not name accumulation.

Possible problem fields include:

- sentence and consciousness
- memory and narrative time
- social perception
- style and delay
- the room / interiority
- jealousy and interpretation
- art and revelation
- habit and shock
- translation and prose rhythm
