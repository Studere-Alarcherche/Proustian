Chapter 003 — Word Families and Recognition Patterns

Before You Begin

Chapter 001 gave you footholds. Chapter 002 taught you to test them. Chapter 003 adds a new move: when you meet an unfamiliar word, look for its family before opening the dictionary.

A word family lets you read more than you have memorized.

A family does not guarantee meaning. It gives you a field of possible meaning.

What You Will Learn

You will learn to:

  • recognize related French forms;
  • use suffixes and prefixes as reading clues;
  • notice noun, adjective, and adverb tendencies;
  • build a small family before choosing a dictionary meaning;
  • read abstract vocabulary without treating every word as isolated.

Core Reading Move

Build a family before opening the dictionary.

The action is:

  1. Notice the base or shared shape.
  2. Gather nearby family forms.
  3. Guess the word type.
  4. Make a cautious reading.
  5. Verify in the sentence.

1. Explanation

A word family is a group of related forms. The forms may not mean the same thing, but they help each other become visible.

Compare:

FamilyReading field
mémoire / mémorable / mémorisermemory, memorable, to memorize
sentir / sensation / sensible / sensibilitésensing, sensation, perceptible, sensitivity
voir / visible / vision / invisibleseeing, visible, vision, invisible
écrire / écriture / écrivain / inscriptionwriting, script, writer, inscription
lire / lecture / lisible / illisiblereading, readable, unreadable

You do not need full verb conjugations yet. You need to recognize that forms belong together.

A0 reading is often a matter of reducing loneliness. A word is less lonely when you can place it inside a family.

4. Pattern Work

PATTERN-Word-Family-Expansion

Pattern

One known form helps you recognize related forms.

Reading Move

Build a small family before isolating the word.

What It Usually Does

It turns an unknown word into a related word: lecture connects to lire; visible connects to voir.

Do Not

Do not assume every family member has the same meaning or force.

Mini Example

une image invisible → visible / invisible belongs to the seeing family.

Where It Returns

Vocabulary banks, Pattern Cards, and Passage Cards.

PATTERN-Suffix-Recognition

Pattern

A suffix gives a clue about word type and meaning field.

Reading Move

Use -tion, -ité, -able, -ible, and -ment as first sorting tools.

What It Usually Does

It helps identify abstract nouns, adjectives, and some adverbs.

Do Not

Do not treat suffixes as mechanical translation rules.

Mini Example

la visibilité d'une image → -ité marks an abstract quality noun.

Where It Returns

Theory syntax and long abstract sentences.

PATTERN-Noun-Adjective-Adverb-Guess

Pattern

Form clues can suggest whether a word names a thing, describes a thing, or modifies an action.

Reading Move

Make a cautious word-type guess before interpreting.

What It Usually Does

It keeps the sentence organized.

Do Not

Do not replace grammar with guessing. Use the guess to keep reading, then verify.

Mini Example

une sensation perceptible → sensation likely names; perceptible likely describes.

Where It Returns

Chapter 005 sentence skeletons.

5. Mini Reading

La mémoire rend une sensation mémorable. Une image visible devient parfois invisible. L'écriture transforme une impression en inscription. La lecture rend le mouvement de la pensée plus lisible, mais une perception obscure reste parfois imperceptible.

First pass: underline family links.

Second pass: mark nouns and adjectives.

Third pass: write one cautious sentence about what the passage is doing.

6. Reading Notes

This chapter prepares a future reader for abstract prose. Proustian and theoretical writing often builds with families: memory and memorable, perception and perceptible, reading and legibility, image and visibility.

The goal is not etymology. The goal is reading momentum.

Exercise Set 5 — Read by family, not dictionary first

For each phrase, identify the family and give a cautious reading.

  1. une sensation sensible
  2. une image invisible
  3. une écriture lisible
  4. une transformation perceptible
  5. un souvenir mémorable
  6. une distinction obscure
  7. une composition visible
  8. un mouvement imperceptible
  9. une lecture difficile
  10. une inscription claire
Show answer

Review Task — Before the dictionary

Choose five unfamiliar words from this chapter. For each one, write:

  • family;
  • likely word type;
  • cautious support meaning;
  • what the sentence must still verify.

Check Your Answer

A good answer does not need perfect translation. It should show that you can connect forms and avoid treating every word as isolated.

What You Can Now Do

You can read beyond memorized vocabulary. You can build a family, guess word type, and verify meaning in the sentence.
