Chapter 005 — What Is a French Sentence?

Before You Begin

You can now recognize footholds, distrust false confidence, build word families, and read small relation words. Chapter 005 gives those skills a sentence frame.

A French sentence is not a cloud of words. It has a skeleton.

A long Proustian sentence is not magic. It is a sentence with many attachments.

In this chapter you will not learn full French syntax. You will learn the first reading action for any French sentence: find the subject, find the conjugated verb, and delay interpretation.

What You Will Learn

You will learn to identify:

  • subject;
  • conjugated verb;
  • complement;
  • modifier;
  • noun group;
  • opening frame;
  • adjective;
  • prepositional phrase;
  • word order;
  • sentence before interpretation.

Core Reading Move

Find the subject. Find the conjugated verb. Delay interpretation.

The action is:

  1. Ignore the urge to translate immediately.
  2. Find the subject group.
  3. Find the conjugated verb.
  4. Mark the complement if there is one.
  5. Treat the rest as attachments until you can place them.

1. Explanation

The minimal skeleton for now is:

Subject + conjugated verb + complement

Not every sentence has a complement, but this pattern is the first stable model.

Step 1

La mémoire transforme une sensation.

PartFrenchReading role
SubjectLa mémoirewhat the sentence starts from
Conjugated verbtransformethe main action
Complementune sensationwhat is transformed

Step 2

La mémoire transforme une sensation obscure en image.

The skeleton is still:

  • Subject: La mémoire
  • Conjugated verb: transforme
  • Complement: une sensation obscure
  • Attachment / result phrase: en image

Step 3

Dans la chambre, la mémoire transforme une sensation obscure en image.

Now there is an opening frame:

  • Opening frame: Dans la chambre
  • Subject: la mémoire
  • Conjugated verb: transforme
  • Complement: une sensation obscure
  • Attachment / result phrase: en image

The sentence became longer, but the skeleton did not disappear.

2. Recognition

Terms for this chapter

TermWorking meaning
subjectthe noun group the verb is built around
conjugated verbthe verb form carrying the sentence action or state
complementa word or group that completes the verb or phrase
modifiera word or group that adds description, place, time, or manner
noun groupa noun with its article and possible adjective
opening framea phrase before the core sentence, often place or time
adjectivea describing word attached to a noun
prepositional phrasea relation phrase beginning with a word such as dans, de, à, avec, sans, sur

4. Pattern Work

PATTERN-Sentence-Skeleton

Pattern

A sentence has a recoverable skeleton even when it looks long.

Reading Move

Find subject + conjugated verb + complement before interpreting.

What It Usually Does

It prevents long sentences from becoming a blur.

Do Not

Do not translate from left to right before finding the verb.

Mini Example

Dans la chambre, la mémoire transforme une sensation obscure en image. → core: memory transforms a sensation.

Where It Returns

Every Proustian passage and every later syntax chapter.

PATTERN-Subject-Conjugated-Verb

Pattern

The subject and conjugated verb form the first anchor of the sentence.

Reading Move

Locate the noun group and the finite verb.

What It Usually Does

It tells you what the sentence is doing before you chase details.

Do Not

Do not mistake every nearby noun for the subject.

Mini Example

Le silence accompagne l'image. → subject Le silence; verb accompagne.

Where It Returns

All reading skeleton work.

PATTERN-Attachments-Light

Pattern

Extra phrases attach to the skeleton as frames, descriptions, or relation phrases.

Reading Move

Mark attachments lightly without overanalyzing them.

What It Usually Does

It lets you keep the sentence open without drowning in detail.

Do Not

Do not introduce full relative clauses yet. That comes later.

Mini Example

Avec attention, le lecteur relit la phrase dans la chambre. → frame/manner + core + place attachment.

Where It Returns

Longer sentences, Passage Cards, and future Proustian syntax.

5. Mini Reading

Dans la chambre, la mémoire transforme une sensation obscure en image. Le lecteur observe cette image avec attention. Sur la table, un livre reste ouvert. Le silence accompagne la lecture, mais une impression change la perception du moment.

First pass: find each conjugated verb.

Second pass: find each subject.

Third pass: mark opening frames and attachments.

6. Reading Notes

This is the first bridge from vocabulary to sentence. Proustian sentences can become long because they gather frames, noun groups, adjectives, and prepositional phrases. You are not ready to parse all of that yet. You are ready to keep returning to the skeleton.

A long sentence becomes less frightening when you can say: “Here is the core. These other pieces attach to it.”

7. Practice

Exercise Set 4 — Compress the sentence

Reduce each sentence to a skeleton note.

  1. Dans la chambre, la mémoire transforme une sensation obscure en image.
  2. Avec attention, le lecteur relit la phrase difficile.
  3. Sur la table, une image ancienne reste visible.
  4. Sans explication, le souvenir revient dans la mémoire.
  5. La perception du moment change la lecture.
  6. Une sensation presque invisible devient une image claire.
  7. Le silence de la chambre accompagne la pensée.
  8. Comme une impression, l'image revient.
  9. La mémoire de l'enfance transforme le présent.
  10. Le lecteur observe la forme de la phrase.
Show answer

1 memory transforms sensation. 2 reader rereads phrase. 3 image remains visible. 4 memory returns. 5 perception changes reading. 6 sensation becomes image. 7 silence accompanies thought. 8 image returns. 9 memory transforms present. 10 reader observes form.

Exercise Set 5 — Delay interpretation

For each sentence, write the order of operations: subject, verb, complement, attachment.

  1. La mémoire transforme une sensation obscure en image.
  2. Dans la chambre, une impression devient visible.
  3. Le lecteur relit la phrase avec attention.
  4. Sur la page, une distinction reste difficile.
  5. Le temps transforme la perception du souvenir.
Show answer

1 subject La mémoire; verb transforme; complement une sensation obscure; attachment en image. 2 attachment Dans la chambre; subject une impression; verb devient; complement visible. 3 subject Le lecteur; verb relit; complement la phrase; attachment avec attention. 4 attachment Sur la page; subject une distinction; verb reste; complement difficult state. 5 subject Le temps; verb transforme; complement la perception du souvenir.

Review Task — Skeleton before meaning

Choose three sentences from the mini reading. For each one, write:

  • opening frame if any;
  • subject;
  • conjugated verb;
  • complement if any;
  • one attachment.

Check Your Answer

You are succeeding if you can postpone interpretation. Do not ask first, “What does the whole thing mean?” Ask first, “Where is the subject, and where is the conjugated verb?”

What You Can Now Do

You can approach a French sentence as a structure. You can find the core before chasing every word. You can treat length as attachment, not magic.
