Chapter 002 — False Friends and Controlled Guessing

Before You Begin

Chapter 001 trained resemblance as a foothold. This chapter adds discipline. Some words look familiar and still mislead you.

The point is not to distrust French. The point is to slow down your first English reflex.

French-English resemblance is a clue, not proof.

What You Will Learn

You will learn to:

  • distinguish a false friend from a partial cognate;
  • use the sequence Recognize → suspend → verify → reread;
  • check a guess against the sentence;
  • avoid mechanical English substitutions;
  • read a short passage where familiar-looking words create traps.

Core Reading Move

Recognize → suspend → verify → reread.

That means:

  1. Recognize the familiar shape.
  2. Suspend the automatic English guess.
  3. Verify with context, word type, and phrase pattern.
  4. Reread the sentence with the safer meaning.

1. Explanation

A false friend is a word that looks like an English word but usually means something different.

A partial cognate is a word that can share an English meaning in some contexts but has other common meanings too.

The problem is not the first guess. The problem is stopping there.

For Proustian reading, this matters because first impressions are powerful. A sensation, an image, or a remembered scene can seem clear before it has been understood. Your reading habit should match that discipline: notice the first impression, then test it.

A safe reader does not ask, “What English word does this look like?” only. A safe reader asks, “Does that English guess fit this French sentence?”

Exercise Set 3 — Choose the safer sentence reading

  1. Actuellement, le lecteur attend.

    • A. Actually, the reader attends.
    • B. At present, the reader waits.

  2. Il assiste à une scène.

    • A. He assists a scene.
    • B. He is present at a scene.

  3. La lecture devient lente.

    • A. The reading becomes slow.
    • B. The lecture becomes slow.

  4. Une déception transforme le souvenir.

    • A. A disappointment transforms the memory.
    • B. A deception transforms the memory.

  5. Elle demande une explication.

    • A. She asks for an explanation.
    • B. She demands an explanation.

  6. Dans le coin, une image reste visible.

    • A. In the corner, an image remains visible.
    • B. In the coin, an image rests visible.

  7. Cette expérience reste obscure.

    • A. This experience / experiment remains obscure.
    • B. This experiment can only mean a laboratory test.

  8. Le caractère du souvenir change.

    • A. The character / nature of the memory changes.
    • B. The fictional character of the memory changes.
Show answer

1 B; 2 B; 3 A; 4 A; 5 A. demander can be strong in some contexts, but the basic reading action is “ask.” 6 A; 7 A; 8 A.

4. Pattern Work

PATTERN-False-Friend-Alert

Pattern

A French word looks like an English word but usually means something different.

Reading Move

Pause the automatic English guess and verify.

What It Usually Does

It protects you from confident misreading: actuellement, attendre, librairie, déception.

Do Not

Do not punish yourself for the first guess. Correct it by rereading.

Mini Example

Le lecteur attend. → The reader waits, not “attends.”

Where It Returns

Every time resemblance feels too easy.

PATTERN-Partial-Cognate

Pattern

A French word overlaps with English in some contexts but not all.

Reading Move

Keep two or more possible readings open until the sentence narrows them.

What It Usually Does

It prevents over-fixed translation: expérience, figure, caractère, histoire.

Do Not

Do not memorize only one English equivalent.

Mini Example

une expérience étrange may be a strange experience or a strange experiment.

Where It Returns

Theory prose and literary description, where abstract nouns shift force.

PATTERN-Controlled-Guessing

Pattern

A first guess is useful only when it can be tested.

Reading Move

Recognize → suspend → verify → reread.

What It Usually Does

It turns uncertainty into a method instead of panic.

Do Not

Do not leave the first guess untested.

Mini Example

la lecture looks like lecture, but the phrase around it may demand reading.

Where It Returns

All later sentence and passage work.

5. Mini Reading

Read slowly. This passage deliberately contains false friends and partial cognates.

Actuellement, le lecteur attend dans une librairie. Il ne cherche pas une bibliothèque. Il demande un livre sur la mémoire. Dans le coin, une figure reste visible sur la couverture. Cette image provoque une déception légère, puis une envie de lecture. L'expérience est sensible, mais son caractère reste obscur.

First pass: underline familiar-looking words.

Second pass: mark the dangerous ones.

Third pass: write a cautious reading in plain English.

6. Reading Notes

This mini reading is not Proust. It is a training surface for future Proustian reading. The fields are relevant: memory, image, reading, disappointment, desire, perceptible experience, obscure character. Those fields return often in literary and theoretical prose.

The important action is not vocabulary collection. The action is controlled rereading.

7. Practice

Exercise Set 4 — Verify by context

For each phrase, explain what makes the bad guess fail.

  1. dans une librairie
  2. il demande un livre
  3. le lecteur attend
  4. une impression sensible
  5. une longue journée
  6. la location d'une chambre
  7. prévenir le lecteur
  8. une envie de lecture
  9. un habit noir
  10. un délai court
Show answer

1 librairie is a bookstore; books are bought there. 2 demander takes the sense ask for. 3 attendre means wait. 4 sensible means perceptible or sensitive. 5 journée is a day span. 6 location is rental. 7 prévenir means warn or notify. 8 envie is desire, not envy here. 9 habit is clothing. 10 délai is a period / time limit; context decides whether delay is acceptable.

Review Task — Reread the mini reading

Write five dangerous words from the mini reading. For each one, write:

  • first bad guess;
  • safer reading;
  • context clue.

Check Your Answer

You are succeeding if you can slow down without freezing. A false friend should make you more accurate, not more afraid of French.

What You Can Now Do

You can use resemblance with judgment. You can identify high-risk familiar words, hold a guess lightly, and reread after correction.
