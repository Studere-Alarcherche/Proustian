# Pattern Bank

## Purpose

This bank records reusable patterns observed while building Learn to Read Proust [EN-CN].

Patterns should come from actual chapter work. Do not overproduce patterns before passages and chapters are tested.

## Pattern Types

Use three pattern families:

- English Sentence Pattern
- Chinese Recomposition Pattern
- Writing Formation Pattern

## Initial Candidate Patterns

### PATTERN-Delayed-Main-Clause

Type: English Sentence Pattern

Problem: The reader wants the sentence to declare itself before the syntax has arrived.

Move: The main clause is delayed so that attention must remain suspended.

Reader Action: Locate the delayed grammatical arrival before summarizing.

CN Recomposition Action: Preserve enough delay for perception while keeping Chinese readable.

Writing Transfer: Practice postponing statement until perception has accumulated.

Status: candidate

### PATTERN-Inserted-Perception

Type: English Sentence Pattern

Problem: Inserted phrases interrupt the reader's grasp of the main line.

Move: Perception enters through interruption, parenthesis, or apposition.

Reader Action: Separate insertion from the main sentence movement.

CN Recomposition Action: Decide whether the insertion can remain embedded or must be repositioned.

Writing Transfer: Add perception without losing the sentence's direction.

Status: candidate

### PATTERN-Sensation-Before-Explanation

Type: English Sentence Pattern

Problem: The reader explains the passage before registering the sensory trigger.

Move: Sensation arrives before interpretation.

Reader Action: Mark the sensory event before naming its meaning.

CN Recomposition Action: Keep sensation prior to explanation.

Writing Transfer: Begin with sensory pressure rather than abstract statement.

Status: candidate

### PATTERN-CN-Preserve-Delay

Type: Chinese Recomposition Pattern

Problem: Chinese recomposition clarifies too early and destroys delay.

Move: Delay is preserved by controlling clause order, punctuation, and timing of explanation.

Reader Action: Identify what must remain delayed.

CN Recomposition Action: Rebuild the sentence so the delayed element still arrives late.

Writing Transfer: Write Chinese prose that delays conclusion without becoming obscure.

Status: candidate

### PATTERN-CN-Reorder-For-Perception

Type: Chinese Recomposition Pattern

Problem: English order becomes unnatural or misleading in Chinese.

Move: Chinese order is adjusted to preserve the perceptual sequence rather than the surface syntax.

Reader Action: Map perception before drafting.

CN Recomposition Action: Reorder by perceptual arrival.

Writing Transfer: Compose by the order of attention.

Status: candidate

### PATTERN-Write-With-Delay

Type: Writing Formation Pattern

Problem: Prose states too quickly and loses the training value of delay.

Move: The writer delays statement through syntax, sensory accumulation, or withheld explanation.

Reader Action: Notice how delay changes thought.

CN Recomposition Action: Use recomposition choices to study delay.

Writing Transfer: Draft a paragraph whose conclusion arrives after perception.

Status: candidate

## Part I Working Patterns｜First Four-Chapter Prototype

### PATTERN｜Delayed Main Clause

Type: English Sentence Pattern

What it does: Delays the governing clause or main verb so the reader must wait before summarizing or translating.

How to recognize it: The sentence begins with temporal, descriptive, subordinate, or reflective material before the grammatical center becomes clear.

How to practice it: Mark the main clause, main verb, delay points, and return point before drafting Chinese.

Proustian function: Turns reading into suspended attention, making arrival itself part of the experience.

Chinese adaptation: Preserve the delayed arrival where possible; divide only when division keeps the movement of waiting.

Used in: Chapter 1｜Waiting for the Main Clause

Citation Evidence: Use 2-4 verified short anchors showing opening pressure, delay point, and return point.

### PATTERN｜Inserted Perception

Type: English Sentence Pattern

What it does: Inserts perception, qualification, or descriptive pressure into an unfinished sentence movement.

How to recognize it: Bracketed or interruptive material can be removed while the governing sentence still resumes, but the sentence loses perceptual force without it.

How to practice it: Bracket inserted material and draw the return path to the governing structure.

Proustian function: Lets perception enter through interruption rather than through external commentary.

Chinese adaptation: Keep the insertion's pressure through embedding, punctuation, repositioning, or strategic division.

Used in: Chapter 2｜Inserted Phrases and Suspended Attention

Citation Evidence: Use 2-4 verified short anchors showing center, insertion, and return.

### PATTERN｜Extended Clause Chain

Type: English Sentence Pattern

What it does: Extends the sentence through linked clauses, sensory additions, qualifications, or delayed completion.

How to recognize it: The sentence reaches several possible stopping points but continues because perception or thought is still unfolding.

How to practice it: Number each extension, label its function, and test what is lost if the sentence is cut there.

Proustian function: Makes syntax a chamber where perception accumulates before closure.

Chinese adaptation: Preserve continuation through sequence, punctuation, and controlled division rather than automatic shortening.

Used in: Chapter 3｜The Sentence That Refuses to End

Citation Evidence: Use 3-5 verified short anchors showing apparent stopping point, extension chain, and final arrival.

### PATTERN｜Delay as Thought

Type: Writing Formation Pattern

What it does: Uses syntactic delay to make thought form through perception, memory, or qualification.

How to recognize it: The delay changes what the reader understands, not only when the reader understands it.

How to practice it: Compare the original sentence with a direct paraphrase and explain what thought-process the paraphrase loses.

Proustian function: Makes delay a method of consciousness rather than an ornament of style.

Chinese adaptation: Preserve the order of thought formation, even when Chinese requires shorter units.

Used in: Chapter 4｜Delay as a Form of Thought

Citation Evidence: Use 3-5 verified short anchors showing perception, delay mechanism, thought turn, and final pressure.
